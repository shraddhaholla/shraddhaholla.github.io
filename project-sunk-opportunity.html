SUNK COSTS VS OPPORTUNITY COSTS

AMAZON · FORD · TATA MOTORS

Should money already spent influence what a company invests in next? And what does a company sacrifice when it chooses one investment over another?

PROJECT OVERVIEW

This project applies a descriptive and analytical approach using secondary information from company reports, financial statements, academic research and published sources. The page presents the research question, case evidence and interpretation in a concise portfolio format.

The analysis distinguishes reported facts from interpretation. Where internal company decisions or opportunity costs are not publicly quantified, the page does not assign unsupported values.

UNDERSTANDING THE CONCEPTS

Past expenditure versus the value of the next-best alternative.

SUNK COSTPast expenditure already incurred and not recoverable
OPPORTUNITY COSTBenefit forgone by choosing one option over the next-best alternative
RELEVANT COSTFuture cost or benefit that changes between alternatives

Sunk costs should not, by themselves, influence future investment decisions. Opportunity costs matter because capital, production capacity, employee time and management resources committed to one project may no longer be available for another alternative.

The study applies these concepts as an analytical framework. It does not assume that the companies explicitly used the terms “sunk cost” or “opportunity cost” in their internal decision-making.

Historical estimates and reported figures are labelled by source. Opportunity costs are not monetarily quantified where public evidence does not establish a value.
