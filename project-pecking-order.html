PECKING ORDER THEORY IN CORPORATE FINANCING DECISIONS

AMAZON VS COCA-COLA

How do two global companies finance growth, investment and operations—and how closely do their choices follow the Pecking Order Theory?

UNDERSTANDING THE THEORY

The financing hierarchy proposed by the theory.

Internal funds → Debt → Equity

01Internal funds — cash generated through operations and retained profits
02Debt financing — loans, borrowings and bonds
03Equity financing — issue of new shares

The Pecking Order Theory, introduced by Myers and Majluf (1984), suggests that companies generally prefer internally generated funds first, followed by debt, and then equity. The hierarchy is influenced by information asymmetry between company management and outside investors.

The theory is a useful explanatory framework, not a strict rule. Financing choices also depend on cash availability, investment requirements, profitability and business conditions.
